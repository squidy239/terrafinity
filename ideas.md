## Light bricks vs CSM sun (deep)
Grounding: the brick proposal (`src/Renderer/vulkan/vulkan_renderer_improvements.md`) keeps the **sun as an unchanged directional `sun_day` term with no CSM** and adds CPU-baked sparse chunk-local 3D light bricks (ChunkSize=32, sample spacing 4, phase 2, `brick_size=11`, 11^3 RGBA16F ~10.6 KB per chunk-layer, one 3D-texture layer per chunk, sampled once per fragment). The current CSM (`src/Renderer/vulkan/shadow/Csm.zig` + `ShadowRenderer.zig`) is a 12-cascade PSSM (`min_split_radius=4` to `max_shadow_distance=128000`, `pssm_lambda=1`, 4096 maps) with texel snapping (`snapCenter`), concentric camera-centered spheres (rotation-invariant), staleness padding from measured `per_frame_dist`, latched light basis stepped in ~1-texel increments (`shouldStepLight`/`latchLight`), and a refresh scheduler (`refreshIntervals` 64->512 frames, `nextRefreshSet`, `cascades_per_frame=1` budget, pending/promote sampling discipline). Question: could RTXGI/DDGI-style irradiance bricks REPLACE the CSM for the sun?
| # | Angle | Light bricks for sun | CSM sun (status quo) | Winner |
|---|-------|----------------------|----------------------|--------|
| 1 | Signal fit: directional occlusion vs diffuse cache | Bricks store low-frequency irradiance; DDGI/RTXGI literature is explicit that probes are a diffuse indirect signal and do NOT replace direct-light shadows (sharp sun contact shadows, tree/canopy dapple, cliff-line definition all live above probe Nyquist). A 4-block brick spacing cannot resolve a 1-block wall shadow or a cave mouth; trilinear sampling smears the terminator. | CSM stores exactly the sun signal: per-texel binary visibility along parallel rays, PCF-blurred in world units (`blur_radius=1.0` converted per cascade) with cascade cross-fade (`cascade_blend`) hiding seams. | CSM, decisively. Bricks answer a different question (what light arrives) than shadows ask (is the sun visible). |
| 2 | Leaking at voxel scale | Brick spacing 4 with phase-2 halo means any occluder thinner than ~4 blocks (1-block walls, doors, leaves, fences) falls between samples or averages into the halo; the classic SVO/voxel-GI failure (thin geometry leaks, mip-averaging turns walls semi-transparent). DDGI fixes this only with per-probe depth + Chebyshev visibility tests driven by ray tracing, which the CPU-baked brick design has no equivalent of. | CSM leaks are bounded and tunable: depth/normal bias (`depth_bias_*`, `normal_bias_scale=1.5`) trade acne vs peter-panning per cascade, verified by matrix ground-truth tests (`depth range maps near/far to Vulkan depth`, `rebuilt matrix at new origin`). | CSM. Bricks without a visibility channel leak strictly worse at 1-block voxel scale. |
| 3 | Planet-scale placement and memory | Sun shadows must cover the 128 km LOD band everywhere the camera looks, including far coarse chunks. Bricks are chunk-local layers: every resident chunk pays ~10.6 KB even where the sun is the only light, and empty air chunks still need layers to answer "lit?" at their surfaces. Scaling brick density up to sun-shadow sharpness (sub-block spacing) multiplies memory 64x per halving and reintroduces the dense-grid cost sparsity was meant to avoid. | 12x4096 D32 cascade array is large (~768 MB worst case) but resolution concentrates where it matters via PSSM log distribution, and far cascades refresh rarely (up to 512 frames) so frame cost stays flat at ~1 raster/frame. | CSM at planet scale; bricks win only as a sparse local-light cache (their designed role), not as global sun coverage. |
| 4 | Update rate under sun motion and edits | Sun moves continuously: every brick containing a shadow boundary invalidates every few seconds, and unlike a torch edit (local re-bake of 1 chunk + neighbors) a sun step dirties the whole resident set at once. CPU re-bake of thousands of 11^3 bricks per sun step dwarfs the flood-fill cost bricks were chosen for, and temporal lag (DDGI's known world-space lag on lighting change) visibly drags sun shadows behind time-of-day. | CSM was built for exactly this: latched basis holds the texel grid frozen while the sun drifts (no swimming), steps discretely (~1 near-texel), staleness padding absorbs camera motion between refreshes, and the scheduler spends 1 raster/frame on the most-overdue cascade. Moving sun = free (basis relatch), moving camera = bounded (padding + near-cascade priority). | CSM, by design. Brick invalidation is local-edit-friendly but global-sun-hostile. |
| 5 | Bake/ray cost and fallback hardware | True RTXGI-style bricks need per-probe ray casts (1-2 Mrays/frame class on RTX hardware) plus BVH upkeep; the CPU-baked flood-fill brick avoids rays but then has no visibility term (see angle 2). Either way there is no Apple/MoltenVK-friendly path without a second implementation, whereas depth rasterization runs everywhere including the required vertex-path fallback. | CSM is plain depth rasterization of the existing face buffers through the shadow pipeline; no ray tracing, no new hardware gate, works on every target including MoltenVK. Graceful degradation is already implemented (contiguous-valid-prefix `cascade_count` early-out, `last_cascade_fade` to lit at the map edge, sun-gated disable at night). | CSM on cost and portability. |
| 6 | Quality scaling and failure modes | Brick sun failure is silent and global: wrong everywhere (leaked interiors, glowing caves, missing noon contrast) with no per-pixel fallback. Raising quality means denser bricks everywhere, paid even in empty terrain. | CSM failure is local and diagnosable: acne/peter-pan/seam in one cascade band, fixable with bias/blur/blend knobs and covered by unit tests (snapping exactness, split monotonicity, refresh budget/due-gating, staleness monotonicity). Resolution follows the PSSM ramp, not a uniform grid. | CSM: its artifacts are tunable parameters, bricks' are structural. |
| 7 | What bricks are actually good for (hybrid) | Bricks excel at exactly what the proposal assigns them: sparse local/block lights (torch in a cave bends around corners, zero per-frame cost, one fragment sample, flood-fill re-bake on edit). They also suit sky/ambient/bounce approximation where low frequency is correct. DDGI itself ships as direct-light-shadow-maps PLUS probes, never probes alone. | CSM cannot do bounce, ambient occlusion in caves, or colored blocklight; it answers sun visibility and nothing else. | Hybrid wins: keep both, each on its native signal. |
Recommendation / verdict: WORSE as a replacement, BETTER as a complement. Do not replace the CSM sun with light bricks under any near-term condition: bricks lose on signal fit, 1-block leaking, planet-scale coverage, sun-motion invalidation, hardware cost, and failure modes (angles 1-6). The proposal file already reaches this conclusion (`Sun: unchanged ... No CSM` in the sense of no shadow maps at all is the acknowledged weakness, bricks cover blocklights only). Ship the hybrid: CSM for sun direct visibility (keep the snapping/scheduler/staleness machinery), bricks for blocklights + later sky/ambient/bounce. Revisit replacement only if ALL of these hold simultaneously: (a) bricks gain a visibility channel (per-texel depth/direction + Chebyshev-style test, i.e. real DDGI probes, not baked irradiance), (b) ray-tracing or GPU cone-trace budget exists for probe updates on all target hardware incl. Apple fallback, (c) brick spacing reaches ~1 block near the camera (clipmap/cascade of bricks mirroring the PSSM ramp) without blowing the ~10.6 KB/chunk budget, and (d) the CSM's VRAM (12x4096 array) profiles as the actual bottleneck. Until then, bricks replacing CSM means trading crisp sun shadows everywhere for soft leaked light everywhere.

## Sunlight underwater (deep)

Grounding: water volume lives in transparent_frag.frag as per-face optical depth `td = density * (bg - frag) * slant * sign`, accumulated into accum/volume_weight and resolved in composite_frag.frag as `transmission = exp(-optical_depth)` plus a scatter term `avg_volume_color * scatter_light * (1 - transmission)`. Three invariants bind every method below: (a) absorption is position-independent (`1 - volume_color`, no light term) so entry/exit face pairs cancel exactly; day/night enters only via `OitCompositor.volumeScatterLight` in the composite; the surface term alone may call `sampleShadow` (shadow/shadow.glsl, 16-tap golden-spiral PCF per cascade). (b) Sky pixels hold cleared depth 0.0 (= infinite), so `sky_dist` (256) substitutes the reference for sky only; real backgrounds keep true distance to planet scale, with `sky_dist_max` (2048) clamping sky-referenced distances symmetrically; slant `length(frag_pos)/fragment_depth` converts eye-Z to ray length. (c) Known gap: camera inside water has an exit face but no entry face, so net td goes negative and clamps to zero = no underwater tint; the fix needs a virtual entry term in the composite or raymarched volumes. Question asked: viewed from UNDER water, can the SUN carry distance and light in the volume, and what if it casts LIGHT (forward-scattered in-scatter, no shadow lookup) instead of shadows?

| # | Method | How it works | Fit for this pipeline | Cost / risk |
| - | ------ | ------------ | --------------------- | ---- |
| 1 | Status quo: shadowed sun on the surface term only | transparent_frag.frag already does `light = mix(ambient) + ndl * sun_day * sampleShadow(frag_pos)` for front-facing water faces; the volume absorption stays unshadowed and the composite multiplies a day/night scalar. Sun shades the water skin, never the water body | Baseline, zero risk, and the only method that already respects pair cancellation. But underwater it reads wrong: submerged rock/floor shading gets CSM shadows while the water column between eye and rock gets none, so depth reads flat and looking up at the sun disc shows no brightening | Free; insufficient alone — distance cue comes only from surface fresnel + Beer-Lambert-ish absorption, no directional light in the medium |
| 2 | Analytic sun in-scatter, unshadowed (recommended light term; sun casts LIGHT not shadows) | Add to the composite scatter term a forward-scattering lobe evaluated per pixel from already-available varyings: `phase = HG(dot(view_dir, -sun_dir), g)` with g ~0.6-0.85 for water particulates (or cheap Schlick `phase ~ (1-g^2)/(1+g*cos)^2` avoiding the pow), times `sun_day * sun_color * (1 - transmission)`. No shadow-map lookup, no per-face light: position-independent by construction, so entry/exit pairs still cancel exactly | Best fit for the invariant: it lands in composite_frag.frag next to `volumeScatterLight`, needs only sun_dir (already a push/uniform) + reconstructed view ray, costs ~10 ALU, works at any distance including planet scale, and degrades gracefully at night (sun_day -> 0 leaves the ambient scatter). Gives the "look toward the sun = brighter water" cue that sells being underwater | Loses occlusion: sun glows through rock walls/overhangs since there is no shadow term; mitigate with cheap proxies (method 4 depth fade, method 6 caustics masked by ndl) rather than a real lookup. Single-HG misses seawater backscatter lobe; upgrade to double-HG later for ~2x ALU |
| 3 | Shadowed sun inside the volume integral (sampleShadow per face or per pixel in composite) | Multiply the volume/scatter term by `sampleShadow(...)`: either per transparent face in transparent_frag.frag (like the surface term) or once per pixel in the composite using reconstructed world pos | Rejected for the volume: breaks invariant (a). Entry and exit faces sample light at different points (different cascades/PCF taps), so the pair no longer cancels and leaves a shadow-shaped residue even for constant-thickness water; per-pixel composite sampling is better (one lookup per pixel) but still needs world-pos reconstruction the composite does not have today, plus 16 PCF taps in the fullscreen pass. | 4 | Depth-from-sun transmittance: Beer-Lambert downwelling by water depth | Attenuate the in-scatter/sun term by the fragment's depth below the surface: `sun_through_water = sun_color * exp(-Kd_rgb * depth_below_surface / max(sun_dir.y, eps))`, with per-channel Kd (red >> green > blue; Jerlov water types / diffuse downwelling Kd as the physically grounded table, Monzon et al. 2024). Depth source: water-surface depth vs opaque/scene depth already in the pipeline (opaque_depth_texture), or a world-y surface level uniform for flat seas | Strong fit as the distance cue the question asks for: sun gives BOTH light (method 2 lobe) and distance (this exponential). Position-safe if evaluated in the composite from per-pixel depth (one value per pixel, not per face, so pairs still cancel). Spectral Kd gives the red-first die-off essentially free (one exp3) and makes shallow vs deep reads instantly | Needs a robust depth-below-surface per pixel (surface depth texture or ray-plane for camera-in-volume); grazing sun (`sun_dir.y -> 0`) needs a clamp to avoid infinite path; Kd table is new uniforms but tiny (3 floats or a 1D LUT) |

| 7 | Face-pair integration fixes: virtual entry term vs raymarched volumes | The underwater-tint gap is structural: inside water there is no entry face. Options: (a) virtual entry in the composite — when camera is submerged (scatter_enabled flag path already exists), synthesize `td_entry = density * absorption * dist entrée` from camera pos to the exit face (or to bg), so the pair completes; (b) fullscreen water-depth reconstruction (separate water-surface depth prepass, opaque depth already sampled) giving per-pixel in-water path length directly; (c) full raymarched volume (march the OIT targets or a separate pass) | (a) is the minimal unblocker: one branch in the composite reusing existing td/accum plumbing, keeps f16 writes small (entry is analytic, not a saturated face write), and every sun method above depends on it — without it methods 2/4 light a transmission of ~1 (nothing to scatter against). (b) is the robust form of (a) for non-flat surfaces (waves, overhangs). (c) is overkill while geometry is voxel faces: marching duplicates the face-pair integral at 10-100x cost | (a): small, do first. (b): one extra depth target or reuse of opaque depth + surface plane math. (c): deferred until volumes decouple from faces |
| 8 | Underwater ambient / multiple-scattering floor (diffuse attenuation ambient, Jerlov ambient tint) | Replace/augment the flat `mix(ambient_min, ambient_max, sun_day)` with a depth-tinted veiling light: `ambient_water(depth) = water_tint * exp(-Kd*depth) + deep_blue * (1 - exp(-Kd*depth))`, i.e. shallow keeps sun-tinted scatter, deep asymptotes to dark blue independent of view direction. This is the multiple-scattering residue that single-scatter HG (method 2) cannot produce | Cheap, composite-only, position-safe (function of per-pixel depth, not per-face light). (2) Add unshadowed analytic in-scatter + depth-from-sun transmittance in the composite (methods 2+4 sharing one Kd uniform triple): `scatter += sun_color * HG(cos_view_sun) * exp(-Kd * depth/sun_elev) * sun_day * (1 - transmission)` — zero shadow lookups, zero per-face light terms, pair cancellation intact. (3) Add world-projected animated caustics on submerged opaques masked by the same depth/sun terms (method 6a). (4) Add the depth-tinted ambient floor (method 8). Explicitly do NOT shadow the volume (method 3), do NOT build a froxel grid or screen-space god-ray blur yet (method 5b/c), do NOT raymarch (method 7c) — re-evaluate only when caves/overhangs make unshadowed in-scatter visibly leak through rock, at which point the fix is a single composite shadow tap at reconstructed pos, never per-face shadowed absorption.

## Physics (deep)

Grounding: `Mover.update` (`src/entity/Physics.zig:45-83`) integrates `vel*dt` in 0.4 substeps then loops `pos -= best_mtv; vel[axis]=0`, with `checkCollision` scanning a `(2*ceil(maxSize/2)+1)^3` cube and returning the single deepest MTV (`penetrationForBlock` resolves min-axis only). `Resistance` is one global `vel *= 1-0.1*dt`; `walkMove` (`src/Game.zig:924-966`) snaps `vel[x,z]=wish` grounded else `vel+=wish*0.35*dt`, wish built from full camera front (pitch leaks into walk), ground = `getShortestGroundDistance(pos-0.001) <= 0` on the deepest Y contact. `Block.water.isSolid()==false` (`src/world/Block.zig:39-48`) so water is air: no medium, buoyancy, or drag.

## GPU-driven scene slot reuse: generation tags vs deferred free (open question)

Grounding: `IndirectScene.index_pool` (`src/Renderer/vulkan/gpu.zig:10`) is a LIFO array of raw `u32`; `releaseCandidate` (`:1263`) frees a slot the moment a mesh retires, while `enqueueRetiredMesh`/`publishPending` defer the buffer write and the cull shader reads `persistent.buffer` every frame. Nothing tags a slot with a generation, so a freed-then-reallocated index is indistinguishable from the original (ABA), and `max_allocated_index` only grows (high-water shrink is unimplemented). The current safety argument rests on the frame timeline throttle proving a GPU-idle publication window — but it is undocumented and untested. Question: is a generation-tagged handle needed, or is a per-timeline-value deferred-free queue sufficient, and where is the reuse cutoff (never within `max_frames_in_flight`?).

Options: (a) keep raw slots + a retire queue drained once the last referencing timeline value retires (deletion-queue pattern); (b) pack `(slot:20, generation:12)` handles and have the cull shader validate the tag before dereference; (c) never reuse a slot within `max_frames_in_flight` (per-frame rings), trading memory for zero ABA. Grounds in current postmortems: Khronos timeline-semaphore docs and the Vulkanised 2026 timeline talk (deletion queue + GPU lifetime tracking via submit latches), Evolve's bindless manager recycling descriptors only out-of-flight FIFO via `retire_handle`, and the vkguide/Khronos MDI samples for the persistent-index-buffer model.

## LOD transition stitching for the chunk band (open question)

Grounding: the chunk renderer is an octree where a coarse voxel exactly contains its children and every level shares one face grid (`ChunkRenderer.zig:629-633`: block origin biased by `(scale-1)/2`), and the loader keeps a parent mesh live until `allCoveredChildren` so coarse and fine meshes coexist during refinement (`Game.zig:184-218`). The surface itself is a continuous height field but each level samples it at `32 * 2^level` block spacing, so where a refined chunk meets a still-coarse neighbour the two meshes share grid lines yet disagree on the *surface* they sample: coarse faces sit on the downsampled height, fine faces on the true height, producing T-junction cracks (and normal/lighting discontinuities) at every level boundary. Nothing currently stitches them — mesh_shaders.md only names ocean/voxel shoreline seams, not chunk-LOD seams. Question: is a transition-cell stitch (Transvoxel-style per-boundary lookup), geometry skirts (downward apron), or vertex geomorphing/blending (a vertex interpolates toward its parent cell's sample as the neighbour coarsens) the right fit for this aligned-face-grid octree, given the renderer already has per-chunk transforms and the shadow cull reads the same faces?

For this pipeline: Transvoxel fits marching-cubes/surface-nets meshers and would need new face encodings + lookup tables threaded through the packed-u64 `Face` path and the dense `Mesher`; geomorphing needs per-vertex parent-position data carried in the face instance and a blend factor in the cull/vertex shaders (eats into the 8 B/face budget, but reuses the existing aligned grid and needs no new geometry); skirts are the cheapest crack hider but add faces and read as vertical walls from underneath. Investigate against the "coarse contains children / one face grid" invariant before choosing.

Links: https://transvoxel.org/, https://0fps.net/2013/07/03/chunked-terrain/, https://dexyfex.com/2016/07/14/voxels-and-seamless-lod-transitions/, http://ngildea.blogspot.com/2014/09/dual-contouring-chunked-terrain.html, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2953481_Real-Time_Generation_of_Continuous_Levels_of_Detail_for_Height_Fields

## GPU-driven light culling for the voxel renderer (open question)

Grounding: today the only light is the directional sun (CSM) plus a flat ambient — no point/emissive light list exists in `src/Renderer` (grep finds `sun_day`/`sampleShadow` only). The planned dynamic set is sparse blocklights (torches, emissive blocks, see the light-bricks entry), which are thousands of small-radius point lights in a voxel world. The renderer already runs a GPU-driven compute cull that writes indirect draws from `IndirectScene.persistent` (`src/Renderer/vulkan/gpu.zig`), and has bindless textures but no per-fragment light iteration. Question: which light-assignment structure fits that compute path — clustered forward+ (Olsson 3D froxel grid, lights binned to clusters by sphere/AABB test, per-fragment cluster lookup), a 2D tiled variant, or a GPU-built light BVH traversed in the shading pass — and does the assignment run once per frame (lights are static per chunk) or per cascade/zone?

For this pipeline: clustered forward+ reuses the existing compute-dispatch-and-barrier machinery and the one-pass face shader with no G-buffer (the renderer is forward, not deferred); its cost is a 3D light-index texture + per-cluster index list (bounded by a max-lights-per-cluster cap or a bitmask, `Granite`'s evolution), and it needs light positions uploaded from chunk edits rather than a global accumulator. Tiled is cheaper memory but collapses badly on a 100,000-block view depth (log-Z slices needed); a BVH avoids the cluster grid and scales with actual light count but needs a builder plus traversal cost per pixel in the 8 B/face shader budget. Ground the choice against the existing cull buffer format and the sun-only shading math before committing.

## Temporal anti-aliasing / upscaling for the voxel renderer (open question)

Grounding: the renderer's only AA is MSAA (`RenderOptions.AntiAliasing` -> `createRenderTargets`/`sampleCountFromAA`, up to 8x, resolved into `render_color`); `camera.computeViewProjection` applies no sub-pixel jitter, there is no velocity/motion-vector attachment and no colour-history target. Scene motion is almost entirely the camera — chunk meshes are static and water/grass/cloud displacement is procedural in-shader — so depth-derived camera reprojection covers nearly every pixel without a per-vertex "previous MVP". Nothing anti-aliases the long view distance: a 1-block edge at 100,000 blocks projects to well under a pixel, so MSAA cannot touch it and it shimmers; MSAA also multiplies colour+depth attachment memory at full resolution, which 8x makes costly on this forward pass.

Question: which temporal path fits — hand-rolled TAA (low-discrepancy projection jitter from a Halton/Sobol sequence, reverse reprojection from depth + previous VP, neighbourhood min/max or variance clipping with an unbiased-luminance blend weight), a vendor temporal upscaler (FSR 3.1 Native AA / Quality through the FidelityFX Vulkan backend, which supplies the TAA resolve and optionally renders at reduced resolution), or a spatial FSR1-style upscale as the low-effort fallback? And what does each require of this pipeline: whether TAA resolves before or after the OIT accum/reveal composite, the reactive and Transparency-and-Composition masks for water/particles that write no velocity, where a swapchain blit replaces the existing final output, and whether the technique survives the vertex-path / MoltenVK fallback (AMD's own Vulkan support matrix starts at RDNA and the SDK lists Intel-Arc Vulkan bugs; Apple would need Metal compute through MoltenVK, so a portable hand-written TAA may beat a vendor upscaler for reach).

## Chunk cache eviction policy: CLOCK Nth-Chance vs scan-resistant FIFO queues (open question)

Grounding: `World.ChunkMapType` / `grids` (`World.zig:196-206`) and the generator `terrain_height_cache` (`Terrain.zig:77`, `Voxelgame.zig:19`) all use the Tigerbeetle-derived set-associative cache (`src/libs/Cache.zig`, `src/libs/SetAssociativeCache.zig`): 256 fragments, default layout 16 ways / 8 tag bits / 2 clock bits, eviction by CLOCK Nth-Chance (a way's count increments on `get`, decrements on a set-miss write, evicts at zero). The chunk working set is camera-relative with strong spatial locality crossed by long sequential sweeps of terrain/LOD streaming; the victim-rehand hazards already logged (WRL-3, `skip_victim` re-arming at count 1) show the clock/count interaction is load-bearing, and a miss is expensive rather than free — AGENTS.md benchmarks chunk (re)generation at ~540 µs base and ~6.4 ms with the erosion filter, so hit ratio trades directly against streaming stall time. Question: does this workload reward a scan-resistant policy over CLOCK — S3-FIFO's small probationary FIFO (filters one-hit wonders) feeding a main FIFO, or SIEVE's single insertion-order FIFO plus a hand with lazy promotion/quick demotion — and can either be expressed per set inside the existing set-associative layout while keeping the padding-optimal clock-hand packing the file is built around? Caveat from the primary sources: SIEVE is turn-key on skewed web workloads but is explicitly *not* scan-resistant; the scan-resistant choice is S3-FIFO (or SIEVE-k with a small saturating per-object count, already close to the current `clock_bits = 2` Nth-chance), which is exactly what a camera sweep across new terrain is. Worth grounding against `WRL-3`/`W2STO-1` too, since a policy swap touches the same bounded-victim-retry machinery.

Links: https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi24/presentation/zhang-yazhuo, https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rvinayak/papers/sieve-nsdi24-simpler-than-lru.pdf, https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rvinayak/papers/s3-fifo-sosp-2023-fifo-queues-are-all-you-need-for-cache-eviction.pdf, https://github.com/Thesys-lab/sosp23-s3fifo, https://s3fifo.com/blog/2023/08/01/fifo-queues-are-all-you-need-for-cache-eviction, https://github.com/tigerbeetle/tigerbeetle/blob/main/src/lsm/set_associative_cache.zig

## Deterministic parallel chunk generation and structure placement (open question)

Grounding: `genTerrainHeight` (`src/world/generators/Terrain.zig:935`) is already a pure function of `(params, level, chunk_pos)` — no neighbor reads, and its central differences are computed from world coordinates alone so chunk borders stay seamless (the property that makes order-independent generation possible at all). But `loadChunk` (`World.zig:240`) runs generation synchronously on the caller, and `addChunkToRender` (`Game.zig:1319`) also loads the 6 neighbor faces one at a time (`:1342`); `addChunkToRenderAsync` (`:1386`) already dispatches through `io.async`/`io.concurrent`. At ~540 µs/chunk base and ~6.4 ms with the erosion filter (AGENTS.md), the single-threaded gen+neighbor-face pitch is a direct streaming-stall lever. The obstacle is the second stage: `generateStructures` (`Terrain.zig:1304`) writes into *neighbor* chunks through `World.Editor` behind a per-chunk `structures_generated` flag, so concurrent placement is order-dependent — exactly the case where Minecraft documents that travel order can change which conflicting feature overwrites which. The shared `terrain_height_cache` (`Terrain.zig:77`) is the other hazard: concurrent misses may duplicate work (benign, identical output) but the underlying `SetAssociativeCache` was until now assumed single-threaded. Question: which decomposition yields thread-parallel generation bit-identical to sequential order — (a) pure per-chunk terrain stage on the `Io` pool with structure placement demoted to a separate ordered pass (or made owner-local, since `JitteredGrid.getStructure` already returns a cell's structure from positions at-or-below it), (b) a Minecraft-style frozen proto-chunk pipeline that advances chunks stage-by-stage, or (c) precomputing shared height/erosion tiles that every worker reads — and which RNG discipline (per-cell counter-based seeds like Random123/Philox versus one advancing shared stream) is required so placement cannot depend on scheduling? Verify the `terrain_height_cache` locking and the `World.Editor` neighbor-write path before choosing.
